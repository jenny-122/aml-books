Course Description
Machine learning studies algorithms that build models from data for subsequent use in prediction,
inference, and decision making tasks. Although an active field for the last 60 years, the current
demand as well as trust in machine learning exploded as increasingly more data become available
and the problems needed to be addressed become literally impossible to program directly. In this
a dvanced course we will cover essential algorithms, concepts, and principles of machine learning.
Along with the traditional exposition we will learn how these principles are currently being revisited
thanks to the recent discoveries in the field.

Pre-requisites
• Programming in at least one programming language. Preferably in python, as homework will
be given with expectation of python knowledge.
• Basic knowledge of linear algebra, optimization theory, probability, and statistics. The class is
taught under assumption that you have taken some machine learning, statistics, calculus, probability,
and optimization theory classes and are ready for advanced material.

• The class is taught under assumption that you are either far along in your graduate studies
and/or are ready to act responsibly as a mature graduate student:
– LATEXtypesetting of all your material turned in for grading is assumed
– Ability to code or learn to code in python is assumed.
– Ability to decide and execute whether to typeset your work in LATEXand turn in pdfs
generated thereby or typeset everything including explanations, figures, and equations
in a jupyter notebook is assumed.
– Although I will try to cover every detail you may need for this class, ability to find and
consult any additional material you may require is assumed.
– Ability to initiate communication and ask questions when anything is unclear is also
assumed.
If you think you are not yet at the stage to be comfortable with above, the class may end up
difficult for you for wrong reasons.

Late Assignments
An assignment submitted past due will be given credit reduced by 2 for each past due day (counted
from the official deadline). For example, a homework scored 96% but submitted one day late will
only receive 48% for the grade; the same homework submitted 2 days late will only receive 24%.
In general, all homework will be scored as score/2𝑛 , where 𝑛 ∈ {0, 1, ...} is the number of days past
due.

Missed Exams
Makeup exams are not given. Exceptions to this policy will be made only in case of a family or
medical emergency, which must be clearly documented. NO LAME EXCUSES, PLEASE!

Academic Dishonesty Policy
Students are expected to submit their own work, to report only on what they completed/attended,
and to conduct themselves in an honest manner. Students who do not follow these guidelines will
be handled in a manner explained in the University Catalog. Please read the section on Academic
Honesty and be honest in your submissions for this course.
One aspect of academic honesty is plagiarism. Plagiarism can include one or more of the
following situations:
1. Copying information from another student’s work or from other printed materials and sub­
mitting that work as your own.
2. Using other people’s ideas, words, or data without properly documenting or acknowledging
the outside source.
3. Overusing outside sources without incorporating your own ideas.
Any work containing plagiarized material will be graded as zero and cannot be made
up. In some cases of plagiarism, students will receive a failing grade for the course. (See
the University Catalog or the University website for additional information on academic honesty
and plagiarism.) Take plagiarism seriously, in this and other classes. If you are not sure how to
document something or what information must be documented, let your instructor know so that
you can go over it together.
Students must work individually on the exam without any assistance from persons or things.
Any student found to be cheating on an examination will receive a score of 0 for the exam, and
possibly be given an F in the class. It is the student’s responsibility to protect work from copying.
No outside help is permitted. If a book or paper is used for the homework assignment, it must be
referenced and not copied. Plagiarized work is determined solely by the instructor and teaching
assistants and is graded solely at their discretion.
